# Reading intake links for direction

Intake links tell you what the user wants their page to *feel* like. Read every link for the points below, then play back a three-to-five-line hypothesis in Step 1 of `SKILL.md`. Don't report the checklist; report what you concluded from it.

## For /now pages (theirs or ones they like)

- **Length.** Rough word count. Under 150, 150-300, or long-form.
- **Structure.** Headers? Bullets? One flowing paragraph? Photos?
- **Sections.** Which areas appear (work, building, learning, reading, place, health, family, travel, making), and in what order. The first section is usually what the author thinks matters most.
- **How personal.** Does it name a city? A partner? Health? Money? Feelings? Or does it stay at "what I'm working on"?
- **Tone.** Earnest, dry, funny, poetic, matter-of-fact. Quote one line that shows it.
- **Specificity.** "Learning Rust" vs. "Rewriting my CLI in Rust because the Python one takes four seconds to start."
- **What it says no to.** Any "not taking on X", "on a break from Y".
- **Freshness cues.** Date line, location line, "this is a /now page" footer.

When the user gives several pages they like, look for what they share, not the average of them. If they share nothing obvious, ask: "What do you like about these?" Their answer is the direction.

## For writing samples (blog post, newsletter, thread)

You're reading for **voice**, not for content and not for format. A blog post's structure (narrative, long paragraphs, a callback ending) is the wrong shape for a /now page, even when its voice is exactly right. Say so in the play-back, e.g. "I'll borrow the voice, not the blog's layout."

What to read for:

- Sentence length and rhythm. Short punchy lines? Long winding ones? A mix?
- Person and register. First person? Casual, formal, technical?
- Humour. None, dry, self-deprecating, puns.
- Recurring moves. Opens on an anecdote? Ends on a callback? Rhetorical questions?
- Punctuation habits worth matching (or avoiding).

Write down three to five concrete voice notes. Those go into the output file's direction section, so whoever polishes the draft later has them too.

## When intake is empty

No links is fine. Default direction: under 250 words, a few short headers, first person, plain and warm, city-level location at most. Say so in the play-back, so the user can push it somewhere else.
